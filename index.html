Defragmentation — the swap book

Trading fragments ownership. After a month of spot fills everyone holds confetti — a few points in each of forty cells — and the market's aggregate bisection has collapsed even though every individual position looks fine. A swap fixes it with no cash: I give you my orphan in your cell, you give me yours in mine. A swap is only offered when both sides gain, because otherwise nobody takes the other side.

Balance

The balancer is the part that pays for itself. Premium above the target utilisation band, discount below it — and the discount is not charity, it is cheaper than the alternative.

Two-sided pricing on/off

Watch the Gini. Nothing in the loop pushes utilisation toward the band directly. A node's target is set by its price relative to the median of its own hardware class, and that price came from its utilisation. The band is where the loop settles, not where it is aimed — which is why the dispersion falling is a finding rather than an animation. Turn the balancer off and watch it climb.

Utilisation distribution

Why dispersion is the thing to minimise

Thermal cycling, not duty cycle, is what kills silicon. A node held flat at 70% wears slower than one swinging between idle and pinned at the same average. So an evenly loaded fleet does not merely use its capital better — it physically lasts longer, and its service events arrive spread out instead of in a clump you have to staff for.

Distance from the thermodynamic floor

orders of magnitude
The one irreversible step in a Clifford datapath is syndrome extraction: 58 qutrits per cycle at kT ln 3 each, which is 2.64 × 10⁻¹⁹ J/cycle at 300 K. Everything else is unitary and therefore Landauer-free. That number is the honest denominator for an efficiency score, because it is a law rather than a benchmark — and today's silicon sits six to seven orders above it.
Hardware classJ/opDecades above floor
A thermodynamic lower bound, not a measured device efficiency. Real hardware dissipates orders more through optical loss, detector inefficiency and classical control. The headroom is a statement about limits and architecture; no full build exists.

Assets & UOR

Liquidity is not a statistic gathered from the tape here. It is a computable property of the asset's own symmetry: value = orbit-stabilizer co-volume.

The address

2⁶⁴ = 40 × 1,296 × payload
The forty vertices of W(3,3) are in canonical bijection with the forty Sylow-3 subgroups of Sp(4,𝔽₃), so an object reference is a pair (P, x): a Sylow subgroup and a coset representative. 40 × 1,296 = 51,840 = |Aut(W(3,3))|. An asset's identity and its position in the fabric are the same 64 bits — there is no separate registry mapping ids to locations that can go stale, and no lookup to do.

Liquidity by co-volume

|orbit| = |G| / |Stab|
Every real-world constraint is a symmetry the asset does not have. A node pinned by data-residency law cannot be moved by the automorphisms that would move it, so its stabiliser grows and its orbit — the set of positions a counterparty could take delivery at — shrinks by exactly that factor. That is the honest reason a residency-pinned node should not trade at the same price as an identical unpinned one.
